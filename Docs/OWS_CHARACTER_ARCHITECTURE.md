# OWS Character Architecture and Independent Conformance Contract

> **Status:** Accepted Stage 3 Character-domain target architecture and independent conformance contract for [issue #107](https://github.com/GameForgeStudio/Unreal-Open-World-Starter/issues/107), approved by Aurora on 2026-08-26 as part of the coordinated #107/#108 architecture package. It defines future OWS behavior and ownership; it does not claim that the target is implemented, authorize gameplay work, or make an implementation child ready for checkout.

## Purpose and controlling sources

The complete maintained GASPALS fork evolves in place into the first-party OWS Character domain. This contract defines the Character side of that evolution without turning GASPALS into an external prerequisite, selecting only convenient inherited pieces, or making Character depend on Vehicle or Character–Vehicle Integration.

This contract is read with:

- the [OWS Platform Architecture](OWS_PLATFORM_ARCHITECTURE.md) for the maintained-fork model, dependency rules, classifications, content tiers, compatibility, and reusable-product conformance;
- the [OWS Shared Gameplay Spine](OWS_GAMEPLAY_SPINE_ARCHITECTURE.md) for identity, semantic Actions, authority, vitality, inventory/equipment, persistence, and shared failure semantics;
- the [OWS Mobility Program Charter](OWS_MOBILITY_PROGRAM_CHARTER.md), [Mobility Architecture](OWS_MOBILITY_ARCHITECTURE.md), and [Mobility Acceptance Matrix](OWS_MOBILITY_ACCEPTANCE_MATRIX.md) for the accepted Character contribution to coordinated Mobility; and
- the [Character–Vehicle Domain Reconciliation Matrix](OWS_CHARACTER_VEHICLE_DOMAIN_MATRIX.md) for exact cross-domain ownership and dependency direction shared with the Vehicle contract.

Within Character scope, this is the specific domain contract. It refines but never silently overrides an accepted package-wide, Shared Gameplay, Mobility, City, or other domain contract. Aurora's latest explicit requirement remains controlling. A real conflict returns to Aurora.

## Document authority and scope

This document is the designated Character-domain authority for:

- the OWS Character product and extension boundary;
- classification of inherited GASPALS capability families and OWS-facing replacements;
- Character-owned runtime, editor, validation, test, configuration, and content responsibilities;
- Character-only build, load, cook, package, networking, synthetic-control, and migration conformance; and
- the Character contribution consumed by the narrow Character–Vehicle Integration family.

It is not authoritative for Vehicle simulation or records, cross-domain relationships, device input routing, world-target selection, UI presentation, shared identity/action/vitality/equipment/persistence semantics, Combat, Hacking, City, or Living World policy.

## Product and composition contract

1. OWS ships the complete maintained GASPALS foundation inside the version-matched OWS bundle.
2. GASPALS evolves in place into the standard OWS Character family. It receives no permanent wrapper plugin and is not a separately installed or updated provider.
3. The standard Character runtime builds, loads, cooks, packages, and conforms without OWS Vehicle or Character–Vehicle Integration.
4. New Supported surfaces use OWS-facing identities and contracts. Inherited names may remain Internal or deprecated for compatibility but do not define the new public contract by accidental exposure.
5. A project may replace the standard implementation only through the documented Supported Character contract and its independent conformance requirements. Replacement does not make inherited GASPALS internals public.
6. The starter shell composes and demonstrates Character. Reusable Character ownership cannot remain in `Source/OWS` or `/Game/OWS` merely because the current demonstration consumes it there.

## Character ownership boundary

| Character owns | Character consumes | Character does not own |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bodily locomotion and execution; pose and stance geometry; traversal; support-relative Character movement; balance, Grip, reach, physical hand availability, attachment affordances, edge-catch and recovery capability; falling, landing, rolling or tumbling; Character animation, embodiment and camera capability; Character-side prediction and correction consumption; Character semantic actions and default mappings; Character authoring, validation, telemetry, and tests | Platform and approved Shared contracts; Supported Input registration/routing; provider-neutral support and transition requests; accepted observations of Equipment-owned assignment/manifestation/slot occupancy, vitality/stamina, damage, and environment context | Vehicle classes or simulation; seats, portals, occupancy or control records; Mobility relationships or reservations; player-device routing; target selection; player-facing/runtime UI widgets; durable inventory/equipment or authoritative Equipment occupancy; shared vitality/damage authority; Combat or Hacking rules; City or Living World policy. Character may own Editor-only authoring/validation UI behind its Editor boundary |

Character executes bodily motion and presentation. It never treats presentation state as physical authority and never learns a Vehicle implementation type to perform Mobility work.

## Public surface and implementation separation

The completed Character catalog must explicitly classify every exposed surface under the Platform contract:

| Classification | Character-domain meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Supported | Stable OWS-facing game-developer contract with migration and declared conformance guarantees |
| Experimental | Available for evaluation with documented limits and no silent promotion into a support claim |
| Internal | Maintained implementation, compatibility, demonstration, or tooling detail that downstream projects are not required to consume |

Deprecation is a lifecycle state, not a fourth support classification. A deprecated Supported surface retains its Supported migration guarantees through the Platform deprecation window; an inherited Internal or Experimental surface does not become Supported merely because a replacement is planned.

Enabled plugins, Blueprint visibility, inherited asset paths, vendor descriptor flags, current demo use, or a successful editor load do not by themselves make a surface Supported.

## Audited inherited foundation

The #107 audit at baseline commit `393b5a34308f5bdbc8d6a52c85748e0df4405c46` records a content-only GASPALS plugin with 2,095 tracked files and 2,090 content assets. The principal inherited families are the Character and Anim Blueprints, CMC movement and pre-CMC updates, motion matching and Pose Search data, traversal traces/choosers/animations, camera rigs, fall/ragdoll/get-up content, Foley, overlays, Enhanced Input assets, retargeted Characters, MetaHuman content, authoring utilities, widgets, and demonstration content.

GASPALS currently has no native runtime module, public OWS API module, domain-local automation suite, test-content boundary, or independent clean-host conformance evidence. Current OWS content derives from and directly references inherited GASPALS assets. Those are protected implementation facts and migration inputs, not proof that every inherited function, property, asset path, tag, input context, camera mode, or demonstration is Supported.

## Overlay, equipment, Combat, and Hacking boundary

Here **overlay** means a Character animation/body-presentation layer. It does not mean an Interaction highlight, a UI or diagnostic overlay, or an authoritative gameplay state.

The inherited overlay audit contains 193 assets: core layer interfaces and chooser tables; four base-profile families (`ALS`, `GASP`, `Masculine`, and `Feminine`); eleven pose families (`Default`, `Rifle`, `Bow`, `Pistol1H`, `Pistol2H`, `Torch`, `Binoculars`, `Box`, `Barrel`, `Injured`, and `HandsTied`); 22 prop assets; six selector-widget assets; transition assets; and authoring animation modifiers. The inherited pose data combines animation recipes with optional meshes, a weapon AnimBP, hand choice, and literal socket names, while the sandbox Character directly selects and replicates overlay state and spawns/attaches those meshes. That coupling is preserved as current behavior and migration evidence, not accepted as the public ownership model.

### Cross-domain ownership and request flow

| Concern | Authoritative owner | Character responsibility |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Body animation, pose execution, layer blending, stance/gait variants, and bodily aim/camera presentation | Character | Validate and execute one resolved body-presentation result; publish availability and typed failures |
| Durable item, equipment host/loadout/scoped slot, assignment, quantity/ammunition/durability/modifications, grants, and persistence | Shared Gameplay inventory/equipment; maintained Sigil is the standard implementation | Consume only public capability and manifestation observations; never copy durable item or assignment state |
| Visible weapon, clothing, tool, carried object, or mounted Actor/component lifecycle and attachment | Shared Equipment manifestation contract; a separately owned host adapter may depend on both Equipment and Character public contracts | Publish neutral body attachment affordances and pose compatibility; never spawn an authoritative item manifestation or infer equipment from a mesh |
| Weapon-ready state, weapon aim validity/solution, fire, reload, ballistics, reactions, and other combat policy | Future Combat contract after its roadmap gate | Continue to own the generic Character Aim action/default mapping and bodily/camera execution; consume a validated provider-neutral Combat presentation request without knowing weapon internals |
| Hacking capability, validation, resources, execution, consequences, and hacking-specific presentation policy | Future Hacking contract after Aurora opens and accepts that program | Expose the same neutral recipe request/result surface; define no hacking behavior, target selector, or special overlay today |
| World focus, selection, highlight/availability routing, and Activate dispatch | Interaction | Publish only Character affordances required by its public contract; never treat an overlay selector or binocular pose as Interaction authority |
| Character–Vehicle relationship presentation context | Integration | Execute an Integration-selected validated recipe through the existing Character presentation seam; Equipment state and Combat/Hacking policy remain outside Integration |
| User-facing selectors/settings and diagnostic presentation | UI or developer tooling | Publish read-only recipe/capability state where accepted; own no selector widget, device key, or debug-overlay policy in Character Runtime |
| Vitality, injury, restraint, mission, or other gameplay truth | The accepted Shared or future domain contract for that truth | Render only an authoritative public observation/request; an `Injured` or `HandsTied` recipe never creates the state it depicts |

Within this overlay/equipment seam, the public dependency remains inverted: Character depends on Platform and approved Shared contracts and may consume Equipment-owned assignment, manifestation, and slot-occupancy observations only through those Shared contracts; its complete domain boundary also permits the Supported Input registration/routing edge declared above. Equipment occupancy and Character-derived physical hand availability/attachment affordance are separate fields with separate owners and cannot overwrite one another. When actual manifestation attachment or another cross-domain edge requires both sides, a separately owned adapter may depend on their Supported public contracts. Authorized higher-layer Combat, Hacking, Integration, project-domain adapters, and synthetic fixtures may submit provider-neutral Character recipe requests through an accepted acyclic dependency. Interaction continues to own selection/Activate only. UI observes state and requests an accepted action or setting rather than issuing unrestricted recipe requests. Character does not link to any consumer's private types, data, assets, or rules.

The governed flow is:

1. The authoritative owner validates and commits—or prepares to commit—its gameplay action/state through the Shared lifecycle.
2. Shared Equipment creates or rebuilds a visible manifestation when an equipment assignment requires one; a mesh or Actor never becomes the durable item.
3. An authorized higher-layer requester submits a source-scoped, revisioned, provider-neutral body-presentation request containing only accepted semantics, stable source/action/item-or-manifestation references, and declared capability/attachment requirements—not requester-authored Character observations, an inherited enum, class, object path, socket, or graph call.
4. Character derives and validates its own current stance, motion, hands, body profile, attachment affordances, recipe availability, and request compatibility, resolves one presentation result, and executes only body presentation.
5. Character returns a typed result so the owner can preserve, reject, interrupt, or degrade only the capability it controls. Animation completion never commits equipment, damage, restraint, hacking, Interaction, or another domain's state.

Exactly one Character presentation policy/provider is active for each declared layer or channel. Simultaneous compatible requests compose only where an accepted recipe declares composition. Duplicate, stale, out-of-order, canceled, or torn-down sources cannot win through last-write order or leave a stale pose. Incompatible required requests do not race or use call order: Character reports an explicit conflict until the relevant integration contract supplies an accepted deterministic policy. No future domain receives implicit priority from its plugin load order, asset name, tag order, or current Blueprint wiring.

### Failure, lifecycle, and proof boundary

- If attachment or pose compatibility is a required precondition, the originating action rejects before commitment with no item loss, partial equip, grant leak, or stale presentation.
- If a durable equipment assignment remains valid while an optional manifestation or presentation asset is missing, durable state survives; the optional visual is omitted or rebuilt under the Equipment contract and the failure is observable. Character never silently unequips, substitutes an unrelated pose, or fabricates a prop.
- A Combat, Hacking, Integration, or other action that requires a missing recipe blocks only that action/transition. An optional cosmetic layer may use only its explicitly declared no-op, never a semantically different fallback.
- Avatar change tears down and rebuilds Equipment-owned manifestations while Character recomputes body compatibility; neither side duplicates the other side's lifetime state.
- Character-only synthetic conformance can certify recipe resolution, all declared recipe variants, provider replacement/multiplicity, player/NPC/neutral parity, missing/conflicting requests, late-join state for an advertised network profile, and the invariant that presentation cannot mutate gameplay.
- Character-only evidence cannot certify equipment assignment/manifestation, weapon behavior, injury/restraint gameplay, Hacking, Interaction, or full Mobility behavior. Those claims require their owning domain and end-to-end tests after the applicable roadmap gate.

The general overlay compositor/catalog is broader than the Mobility-only scope of #153 and that issue explicitly excludes animation-asset authoring. Its implementation and independent conformance belong to the separately gated [#170 — OWS Character overlay recipe compositor and bounded presentation catalog](https://github.com/GameForgeStudio/Unreal-Open-World-Starter/issues/170); recording that child does not make it ready.

## Semantic actions and control-source parity

Character owns the runtime meaning and default mappings of its Supported semantic actions and registers them through the Supported Input contract. Input alone owns device discovery, keys/buttons, modifiers, triggers, context stacks, priorities, conflict resolution, rebinding and accessibility routing. Character never removes another domain's context or polls a physical key in Supported runtime code.

The public control path accepts device-independent values and governed discrete Actions. Player Input, NPC logic, server policy, automation and project-defined providers submit the same semantics; an NPC does not emulate a keyboard or call a private Character graph. Continuous Move/Look and maintained bodily controls carry bounded values, while discrete changes use the Shared Action lifecycle when they can be rejected, interrupted, duplicated, timed out, or authoritatively committed.

The present GASPALS `IA_Interact` and `IA_NextCharacter` assets remain compatibility/demo inputs rather than Character-owned Supported actions, and the present `IA_Ragdoll` is Experimental. Canonical semantic ownership is split explicitly: Character owns its Character Actions and the optional ragdoll meaning, Interaction under #118 owns Activate, and the starter shell owns roster cycling; Input under #117 owns physical contexts, bindings, priorities, routing, and rebinding. Applicable exact #121 inventories must decide whether each current asset migrates, maps, remains as compatibility, or retires and must assign one physical owner/tier without presupposing that `IA_Interact` equals OWS Activate. The documented player controls remain unchanged until those owner-scoped migrations pass regression coverage.

## Character provider and replacement contract

The completed Supported public surface provides Blueprint and C++ paths, where Unreal permits, for:

- observing Character identity, locomotion, pose/stance, movement mode, support response, reach/free-hand, fall/roll/recovery and presentation capability without reading an inherited Blueprint;
- submitting semantic control and provider-neutral execution requests and receiving governed results/failure reasons;
- consuming one optional authoritative stamina capability through the accepted Shared contract; the standard Character package may include a disabled-by-default adapter or policy, but never a second stamina/vitality attribute authority;
- publishing provider-neutral support, pose, reach, free-hand, balance, Grip and edge-catch capability through one validated Character provider seam;
- selecting one active animation provider, one presentation policy/provider per declared layer or channel, and one embodiment and camera provider per authoritative role, with clear failure for missing or duplicate providers;
- executing Integration-selected context through a replaceable Character-owned recipe/alignment execution seam; Motion Warping is the default Character adapter, while Integration owns relationship path/target/timing resolution and authoritative Character and relationship state remain provider-independent;
- using synthetic support, transition, control and presentation providers in Character-only conformance; and
- replacing the standard Character implementation while retaining stable OWS-facing identities, schemas, Actions, authority rules, diagnostics and migration behavior.

Provider selection is a startup/composition-time contract. Runtime hot-swap is not promised. An alternative provider advertises only the capability rows it passes. A replacement may omit an optional feature by explicit manifest and deterministic failure; it cannot claim the complete default Character profile while silently dropping a Target Supported family.

## Character support-claim gates

No Target Supported family is advertised until all applicable evidence passes:

1. Character Runtime builds and loads in a minimal host with Vehicle and Integration absent and no forbidden `/Game` or peer-private reference.
2. The selected Character profile cooks, packages, launches, and excludes Editor/Test/development-only surfaces from Shipping.
3. Supported Blueprint and C++ contracts compile, validate provider multiplicity, report missing optional capabilities clearly, and migrate without data loss.

## Relationship to coordinated Mobility

Character accepts provider-neutral support, load, contact, hazard, and transition-execution requests and returns governed Character results. Character never knows Vehicle seats, doors, graph records, reservations, or concrete providers. Integration owns the relationship and selects validated context; Character remains the sole executor of bodily locomotion, animation, embodiment, fall/roll/recovery, and the Character camera capability.

Character and Vehicle are designed in one coordinated architecture pass because their public contracts must compose. They remain independent products and test targets; neither contract is a private extension of the other.

### Character-side consequence and destruction boundary

Physical response and vitality mutation are orthogonal. Character may begin a measurement-driven brace, slide, fall, roll, tumble, or recovery from authoritative contact evidence even when its profile has no vitality provider or a Shared transaction produces no damage. Conversely, a Shared vitality result cannot move the body, select an animation timer as physics, or commit a Mobility relationship.

For an acknowledged Character–Vehicle relationship, #21/Integration emits one immutable load/hazard context and deduplicates the physical coupling path. Shared #128 validates and mutates a participating Character's vitality exactly once on the server. Character consumes the authoritative result and owns its bodily/presentation response. Vehicle supplies point/force evidence and applies accepted forces; it never reads or mutates Character vitality, infers occupant damage from Vehicle damage, or iterates private occupancy to apply health effects.

Vehicle part/body/provider invalidation reaches Character only through the committed public Vehicle revision and Integration interruption. Integration commits the relationship release from the last valid same-time point motion; Character executes the resulting bodily failure/recovery branch and applies each accepted new physical impulse once. Character does not keep a stale attachment or repair/revive the Vehicle. Character-only synthetic proof can certify this consumer behavior but cannot certify Vehicle destruction, Shared vitality, Integration interruption, Combat consequences, or complete Mobility.
