# OWS Vehicle and Driving Architecture and Independent Conformance Contract

> **Status:** Accepted Stage 3 Vehicle-domain target architecture and independent conformance contract for [issue #108](https://github.com/GameForgeStudio/Unreal-Open-World-Starter/issues/108), approved by Aurora on 2026-08-26 as part of the coordinated #107/#108 architecture package. It defines future OWS behavior and ownership; it does not claim that the target is implemented, authorize gameplay work, or make an implementation child ready for checkout.

## Purpose and controlling sources

The complete maintained KinetiForge fork evolves in place into the first-party OWS Vehicle domain. This contract defines the Vehicle and driving side of that evolution while preserving the accepted Forza-style goal, the complete current road-Vehicle baseline, and independent composition without Character or Character–Vehicle Integration.

This contract is read with:

- the [OWS Platform Architecture](OWS_PLATFORM_ARCHITECTURE.md) for the maintained-fork model, dependency rules, classifications, content tiers, compatibility, and reusable-product conformance;
- the [OWS Shared Gameplay Spine](OWS_GAMEPLAY_SPINE_ARCHITECTURE.md) for identity, semantic Actions, authority, vitality, persistence, and shared failure semantics;
- the [OWS Mobility Program Charter](OWS_MOBILITY_PROGRAM_CHARTER.md), [Mobility Architecture](OWS_MOBILITY_ARCHITECTURE.md), and [Mobility Acceptance Matrix](OWS_MOBILITY_ACCEPTANCE_MATRIX.md) for the accepted Vehicle contribution to coordinated Mobility; and
- the [Character–Vehicle Domain Reconciliation Matrix](OWS_CHARACTER_VEHICLE_DOMAIN_MATRIX.md) for exact cross-domain ownership and dependency direction shared with the Character contract.

Within Vehicle scope, this is the specific domain contract. It refines but never silently overrides an accepted package-wide, Shared Gameplay, Mobility, City, or other domain contract. Aurora's latest explicit requirement remains controlling. A real conflict returns to Aurora.

## Document authority and scope

This document is the designated Vehicle-domain authority for:

- the OWS Vehicle/driving product and extension boundary;
- classification of inherited KinetiForge capability families and OWS-facing replacements;
- Vehicle-owned runtime, editor, validation, test, configuration, and content responsibilities;
- Vehicle-only build, load, cook, package, networking, synthetic-consumer, and migration conformance; and
- the Vehicle contribution consumed by the narrow Character–Vehicle Integration family.

It is not authoritative for Character bodily state, cross-domain relationships, device input routing, world-target selection, UI presentation, shared identity/action/vitality/persistence semantics, Combat, Hacking, City generation, or Living World traffic policy.

## Product and composition contract

1. OWS ships the complete maintained KinetiForge foundation inside the version-matched OWS bundle.
2. KinetiForge evolves in place into the standard OWS Vehicle family. It receives no permanent wrapper plugin and is not a separately installed or updated provider.
3. The standard Vehicle runtime builds, loads, cooks, packages, and conforms without OWS Character or Character–Vehicle Integration.
4. New Supported surfaces use OWS-facing identities and contracts. Inherited names may remain Internal or deprecated for compatibility but do not define the new public contract by accidental exposure.
5. The maintained current Vehicle foundation remains the first supported baseline. Replacement requires measured benefit, compatibility and migration evidence, and Aurora's explicit approval.
6. The starter shell composes and demonstrates Vehicle. Reusable Vehicle ownership cannot remain in `Source/OWS` or `/Game/OWS` merely because the current demonstration consumes it there.

## Vehicle ownership boundary

| Vehicle owns | Vehicle consumes | Vehicle does not own |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vehicle simulation and force application; collision bodies, mass and physical materials; driving behavior; exact Vehicle point kinematics and history; stable scoped body/zone/edge/anchor/portal/seat/control identities; Vehicle graph contributions; capacities and compatibility; canonical occupancy and control records; structural animation; Vehicle semantic actions and default mappings; camera anchors/data and driving-specific behavior; tuning, authoring, validation, telemetry, and tests | Platform and approved Shared contracts; Supported Input registration/routing; public world surface/environment observations; provider-neutral force and structural-invalidation requests; accepted authority, identity, persistence, and consequence contracts | Character movement, visibility, animation, embodiment, or bodily state; Mobility relationships or reservations; player-device routing; target selection; UI widgets; shared vitality/damage authority; Combat or Hacking rules; City generation; traffic population, routing, schedules, or Living World policy |

Vehicle applies accepted simulation forces through its public contract. It never moves, hides, animates, attaches, or changes bodily state on a Character.

## Public surface and implementation separation

The completed Vehicle catalog must explicitly classify every exposed surface under the Platform contract:

| Classification | Vehicle-domain meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Supported | Stable OWS-facing game-developer contract with migration and declared conformance guarantees |
| Experimental | Available for evaluation with documented limits and no silent promotion into a support claim |
| Internal | Maintained implementation, compatibility, demonstration, or tooling detail that downstream projects are not required to consume |

Deprecation is a lifecycle state, not a fourth support classification. A deprecated Supported surface retains its Supported migration guarantees through the Platform deprecation window; an inherited Internal or Experimental surface does not become Supported merely because a replacement is planned.

Plugin Beta metadata, C++ header placement, Blueprint exposure, inherited asset paths, current demo use, or an editor stress map do not by themselves make a surface Supported.

## Audited inherited foundation

The #108 audit at baseline commit `393b5a34308f5bdbc8d6a52c85748e0df4405c46` records 398 tracked KinetiForge files, including two runtime modules, 29 headers, 24 C++ implementation files, 333 `.uasset` files, two `.umap` files, and 102 files in the inherited Vehicle-template tree. The maintained foundation includes async-physics execution; a component-based drive assembly; wheel/tire, suspension, axle, engine or electric-motor, clutch, gearbox, differential/transfer-case, aerodynamic, camera, input, tuning, and demonstration surfaces; plus the exact fourteen profiles placed and protected by OWS.

All thirteen specialized roster Blueprints derive from the inherited `DefaultVehicle`, which supplies the common Pawn, physics body, drive assembly, async-physics spring arm/camera, and driving control graph. That current inheritance and every raw KinetiForge component/type remain audit and migration facts, not automatic public support. `BP_PhysicalTruck` also exists in the maintained fork but is not one of the fourteen placed/accepted OWS roster Vehicles; it is an Internal low-level/manual-assembly engineering prototype, not an alternate backend or roster support claim.

### Vehicle profile and control contract

Each roster or project-defined Vehicle has one validated capability/profile manifest. It declares its stable definition identity, standard simulation provider, body/axle/wheel and propulsion categories, applicable semantic controls, tuning/feel profiles, camera and structural capabilities, Mobility-facing point/graph contributions, platform/network claims, and optional features. Exact symbols, schema fields, asset destinations, and numerical values belong to separately accepted implementation/evidence issues.

Common requirements apply to every profile. Optional features are explicit and discoverable; an unsupported request returns a typed result rather than calling a missing component, polling a device, or guessing from the Vehicle class name. A manifest cannot label a capability Supported when its provider, required data, test fixture, migration path, or declared platform/network evidence is absent.

Player Input produces the same Vehicle-owned semantic control values and discrete requests accepted from NPC, server-policy, neutral, and automation sources. Non-player sources never emulate a keyboard/controller or require a Character Pawn. Equal granted control and time inputs traverse the same shaping and simulation path within #41 tolerances; source-specific device sampling or disposable local presentation cannot change the canonical Vehicle result.

Exactly one current control grant/source may drive a Vehicle. Foreign, duplicate, stale, out-of-order, or post-revocation input is rejected without partial force application. Source loss converges to the profile's declared neutral-input policy and cannot invent an occupant, possession, parking brake, engine, recovery, or traffic rule. Vehicle-only conformance exercises this through synthetic Shared identities/records without implementing a Character or Integration relationship.

### Developer-optional Vehicle reset and reposition

OWS includes a Supported semantic Vehicle reset/reposition capability, but the developer's authoritative project/composition profile disables it by default. A developer may leave it disabled, enable the included safe upright-in-place provider, or register one conforming replacement policy. This is a developer product decision synchronized as gameplay configuration, not a device-local or untrusted client preference. Enabling it registers the semantic Action through the accepted Input extension contract; UI may observe the public enabled state and present a developer-selected affordance. This architecture assigns no physical default binding and does not change current controls.

When enabled, the included provider covers every canonical V01–V14 profile; V13's complete articulated assembly and V14's extreme scale are required cases, not silent exclusions. A project-defined road-Vehicle profile explicitly declares compatibility with that provider and passes its reset evidence before exposing the Action for that profile. Unsupported or invalid profile/provider combinations fail validation rather than disappearing at runtime.

The included baseline policy is deliberately narrow:

1. A governed Shared Action request addresses the exact Vehicle and expected revisions. Its authenticated source must hold the current Vehicle control grant/owner authorization or be an authorized developer server-policy source. Only the server validates and commits it; client prediction is limited to reversible presentation. Reset neither infers possession from control nor creates, transfers, or removes possession or control.
2. The Vehicle must be valid, finite, outside terminal Vehicle structural state, and outside an accepted or committing persistent-entity retirement transaction; it must also remain below #41's certified reset motion thresholds and be inverted or immobilized under the enabled policy. A normally controllable moving Vehicle cannot use reset as travel or braking.
3. The provider preserves the current horizontal position and yaw where physically valid, aligns the Vehicle to the authoritative gravity/up frame, and searches only the bounded minimum displacement required for a non-penetrating, supported pose. It never selects a distant checkpoint, roadway, spawn, or arbitrary client transform.
4. The provider validates complete collision clearance, support, slope, streaming/readiness, permissions, and current revisions before mutation. No valid pose produces a typed rejection with no partial transform, velocity, graph, occupancy, control, persistence, or presentation change.
5. On commit, one validated assembly mutation moves every affected rigid or articulated body atomically, preserving declared internal relative pose/constraint state, and zeroes every affected body's linear and angular velocity. Because transform/contact support changes, every moved body wakes for the first post-reset physics step and may sleep again only through ordinary finite solver policy. The provider opens a new kinematic epoch/revision and publishes its first post-reset sample; bounded pre-reset history remains under the prior epoch, and no interpolation, finite difference, rewind, or motion inference crosses the discontinuity. Stable graph schema/IDs and occupancy/control revisions do not change unless their own records change. Identity, control grant, engine, gear, fuel/energy, inventory, vitality, part/body validity, terminal structural state, accepted retirement state, customization, and every other durable field remain unchanged.
6. The Vehicle-only baseline accepts an empty occupancy record with no registered external spatial lease/interlock; a current Vehicle control grant alone may authorize the request under step 1. In combined composition, any occupant, externally composed possession/control relationship, pending reservation, spatial lease, relationship, or in-flight transition requires an Integration-owned interlock/transaction; absent that current authorization, Vehicle returns `coordination required` without mutation. Only a separately accepted Integration recovery transaction may coordinate Character state and then invoke the Vehicle provider. Vehicle never teleports or animates a Character, and reset never infers or mutates possession.
7. The action is idempotent, rate-limited through declared policy, observable through the shared result/diagnostic contract, and replicated from canonical state. The committed spatial mutation follows the normal Vehicle persistence contract while every other durable field remains unchanged. Reconnect or late join cannot replay the request or observe an intermediate pose.

A replacement Vehicle policy may change its declared bounded placement strategy and consume provider-neutral authorization or eligibility observations, but it does not take authority from another domain. Costs, cooldowns, duration, cancellation, or prediction use the selected Shared Action executor, with GAS as the default when those facilities are required; mission and damage truth come from their owning domains through public observations; occupied recovery remains an Integration transaction. A higher-layer adapter may compose those owners without introducing a private Vehicle dependency. Issue #47 selects and proves each additional reset policy under #41 evidence conventions, while #48 owns public tuning values, units, certified ranges, defaults, inheritance, and migration. A replacement cannot claim the included baseline profile while changing its bounded placement rule, repairing damage, regenerating a Vehicle in terminal structural state or accepted/committing retirement, bypassing collision/streaming validation, or moving another domain without that domain's accepted coordination.

### Driving-assist and ADAS boundary

Target Supported basic assists are bounded Vehicle response, not a second driver. Each profile and each `Balanced`, `Cinematic`, or `Simulation` feel profile declares which assists apply and their #41/#48-certified range. They operate only after semantic-control admission, shaping Vehicle response or constraining downstream steering, drive-torque, or brake contribution in one deterministic order under the current control grant; they cannot choose a destination, generate intent, inspect a player device, bypass authority, or silently differ by control-source type. The current locally controlled automatic-gearbox gate is migration evidence and must not survive as source-dependent canonical behavior.

The existing electric Vehicle behavior is likewise separate from unfinished extension points. V08 uses its dedicated electric motor, EV clutch policy, and EV gearbox, while V14 has its own declared electric-torque configuration. Passing those profile rows cannot certify the raw P1-P4 torque injections or ESP brake injection.

The inherited ADAS class is Internal in full. Its timer path is disabled, its tick functions perform no ADAS work, maintained KinetiForge content does not consume it, several helpers mutate raw drive input directly or use synchronous navigation/collision queries, and HumanLike behavior is explicitly marked under development. No current ADAS function may be presented as an Experimental OWS surface merely because callable code remains in the fork.

There are four reserved promotion lanes, none of which is currently advertised or available as an OWS Experimental product surface:

| Possible future lane | Earliest allowed classification | Admission conditions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bounded closed-course speed/control follower derived from inherited cruise/direct/simple behavior | **Experimental** | A separately accepted OWS-facing adapter receives an already authorized bounded target-speed/control constraint and submits through the canonical control grant/path, declares its closed-course limits, excludes HumanLike and traffic/autonomy claims, and passes deterministic authority/network/failure evidence; NavMesh, destination, and route generation remain outside Vehicle ownership |
| Provider-neutral ambient world-space wind-velocity/air-density consumption seam | **Experimental** | A separately accepted Vehicle adapter validates authority, location, world frame, time/revision, units, bounds, absence behavior, and deterministic replication for observations supplied by the owning world/environment system; classification of that external provider remains with its owner, and Vehicle gains no dynamic-weather dependency |
| Additive electric/regen controller provider | **Experimental** | A separately accepted contract defines source/lifetime, torque units and ordering, saturation, applicable energy/battery/thermal accounting, authority, rollback, tuning, and telemetry instead of exposing P1-P4 mutation hooks |
| ESP/stability controller provider | **Experimental** | A separately accepted contract defines source/lifetime, brake contribution units and ordering, saturation, authority, rollback, tuning, and telemetry instead of exposing the raw ESP brake setter |

Promotion from any lane to Supported requires Aurora's explicit approval, migration guarantees, applicable V01-V14/profile evidence, and no weakening of the standard-provider baseline.

### Aerodynamics and environment observations

The first Target Supported roster baseline includes profile-declared self-air drag/downforce under deterministic still-air and profile reference-density conditions. It does not claim a dynamic-weather system. The current coordinator supplies zero wind and components carry raw density values; both remain Internal until an accepted environment-observation contract replaces those assumptions.

Vehicle may eventually consume a public, provider-neutral ambient world-space wind-velocity and air-density observation at an exact location, world frame, and authoritative time/revision from the owning world/environment system. Vehicle derives apparent/relative wind exactly once from that ambient observation and its own exact point velocity. Absence of the optional provider must reproduce the certified still-air baseline, exactly zero or one provider may be authoritative for a sample, and stale, invalid, duplicate, out-of-range, or discontinuous observations must fail or fall back according to an explicit deterministic policy rather than leak raw world or weather dependencies into Vehicle. The section below owns only the Vehicle-facing world-surface consumer port and physical response; City/world retains source and mutation truth, and neither seam makes Vehicle the owner of weather generation, surface authorship, or City policy.

### Simulation-provider and alternative-backend boundary

The maintained KinetiForge-on-Chaos implementation is the sole standard provider for the first Target Supported road-Vehicle family. It evolves in place, and the standard path does not acquire a permanent wrapper solely to conceal the maintained fork. Provider identity, raw types, component layout, fixed-step detail, or literal asset paths do not enter OWS public contracts, save data, semantic results, or downstream content requirements.

There is no current OWS alternative-provider interface and no current Experimental alternative backend. Direct shell probing of both `UModularVehicleBaseComponent` and `UVehicleDriveAssemblyComponent`, enabled Chaos Modular example plugins, and the retained manual-assembly `BP_PhysicalTruck` are Internal compatibility/prototype residue only.

A future alternative begins, at most, as a disabled-by-default Experimental compatibility adapter in its own module. It depends on the OWS Vehicle public contract rather than KinetiForge private surfaces; composition selects exactly one authoritative simulation provider before runtime; and the adapter cannot hot-swap, run dual solvers, silently fall back, or expose backend identity in public APIs or durable state.
